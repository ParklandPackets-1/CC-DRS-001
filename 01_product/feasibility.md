# Feasibility Assessment — Cold-Climate De-Icing Retrofit System (CC-DRS)

## Purpose
This document defines the core technical uncertainties and validation activities required to progress CC-DRS from concept to prototype-ready status. It is intended to support internal decision-making, supplier engagement, and early-stage innovation support discussions (e.g., IRAP, provincial programs).

This is not a commercialization plan.

---

## Problem Statement
In cold-climate operations, vehicle windshield wipers frequently become immobilized due to ice accumulation at the wiper park zone and cowl interface. This results in:
- Reduced driver visibility and safety risk
- Extended winter idling solely to free wipers
- Increased fuel consumption and emissions
- Premature wiper blade damage
- Operational downtime for fleets and municipalities

Existing solutions are limited, non-retrofit-friendly, or fail under extreme cold, salt, vibration, and misuse conditions.

---

## Proposed Solution (Conceptual)
CC-DRS is a retrofit-friendly, low-voltage heating system designed to:
- Thaw ice at the wiper park zone and cowl interface
- Maintain mechanical operability during snowfall and freezing conditions
- Operate safely during unattended modes (e.g., remote start)
- Avoid dependence on cloud connectivity or behavioral change

The system uses a zone-based heating architecture and self-regulating thermal elements, prioritizing mechanical release over cosmetic clearing.

---

## Technical Uncertainties
The following uncertainties must be resolved through supplier evaluation and prototype testing:

### 1. Materials Survivability
- Performance of heater laminates at −40 °C (commercial) and −55 °C (planned Arctic variant)
- Long-term adhesion to glass-adjacent surfaces under salt, washer fluid, and thermal shock
- Encapsulation durability under vibration, abrasion, and chemical exposure

### 2. Electrical & Power Behavior
- Battery voltage sag behavior at extreme cold
- Safe power limits during unattended operation
- Summer misuse survivability under solar load

### 3. Mechanical Robustness
- Resistance to vibration, impact, and user abuse (e.g., manual blade release)
- Stability of heater placement under wind load and snow packing

### 4. Environmental Envelope Validation
- Operation under salt brine, dust, and mixed precipitation
- Cold-start behavior and thaw time consistency

---

## Validation Activities (Planned)
- Supplier datasheet and test data review
- Bench testing of heater, adhesive, and encapsulation samples
- Cold-soak and thermal cycling tests
- Limited vehicle-mounted prototype trials
- Electrical monitoring during unattended and attended operation

---

## Variant Strategy
- **CC-DRS-C (Commercial):** −40 °C operating target for fleets, municipalities, and civilian users
- **CC-DRS-A (Arctic):** Planned −55 °C variant for high-Arctic and defense-adjacent applications

The Arctic variant retains the same core architecture with reinforced materials and expanded validation.

---

## Rationale for Public Support
Early-stage support reduces technical risk in:
- Extreme-environment materials selection
- Safety-critical retrofit validation
- Idle-reduction and emissions-impact measurement

Public support enables validation prior to pilot deployment, without distorting engineering priorities or accelerating premature commercialization.
